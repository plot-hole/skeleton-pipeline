FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS

9. On March 15, 2023, Thompson purchased a Model X-450 Safety Monitoring Device manufactured by Johnson & Johnson from a retail store in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania.

10. The device contained electronic components manufactured by Baker Hughes and was developed with financial consulting services provided by Morgan Stanley.

11. Rodriguez, in his capacity as Vice President of Product Safety, was directly responsible for overseeing the safety testing and approval process for the Model X-450 device.

12. On April 2, 2023, while Thompson was using the device as intended at her residence, the unit suddenly overheated and exploded, causing severe burns to her left hand and arm.

13. The explosion occurred due to a defective lithium battery component manufactured by Baker Hughes, which Rodriguez and Johnson & Johnson failed to properly test despite knowing of similar incidents with comparable devices.

14. As a direct and proximate result of the incident, Thompson sustained second and third-degree burns requiring immediate emergency medical treatment at Thomas Jefferson University Hospital.

15. Thompson's medical expenses to date total $47,832, and she continues to require ongoing physical therapy and reconstructive surgery.

16. The injury has prevented Thompson from performing her duties at Marshall & Associates Inc., resulting in lost wages of approximately $15,200.

17. Thompson has suffered and continues to suffer severe physical pain, emotional distress, and permanent scarring as a result of Defendants' negligent conduct.

COUNT I - STRICT PRODUCTS LIABILITY

18. Thompson incorporates by reference all preceding paragraphs.

19. The Model X-450 device was defective and unreasonably dangerous when it left Defendants' control.

20. Johnson & Johnson, Baker Hughes, and Rodriguez are strictly liable for Thompson's injuries and damages.

COUNT II - NEGLIGENCE

21. Thompson incorporates by reference all preceding paragraphs.

22. Defendants owed Thompson a duty to exercise reasonable care in designing, manufacturing, testing, and marketing the device.

23. Rodriguez breached his duty by failing to implement adequate safety protocols, and Morgan Stanley was negligent in its financial analysis that prioritized cost reduction over safety measures.

24. Said negligence was the proximate cause of Thompson's injuries.

COUNT III - BREACH OF WARRANTY

25. Thompson incorporates by reference all preceding paragraphs.

26. Johnson & Johnson impliedly warranted that the device was fit for its intended purpose and free from defects.

27. The device breached these warranties, causing Thompson's damages.
