COMPLAINT

TO THE HONORABLE COURT:

Plaintiff Maria Elena Martinez, by and through her undersigned counsel, hereby brings this action against Defendant Greenfield Properties LLC for damages arising from uninhabitable living conditions and negligent maintenance of residential rental property, and respectfully alleges as follows:

PARTIES AND JURISDICTION

1. Plaintiff Maria Elena Martinez is an individual residing at 2847 Sycamore Lane, Edison, New Jersey 08817. Her Social Security Number is 412-78-3956, and she may be reached at (732) 548-9021 or m.martinez847@gmail.com.

2. Defendant Greenfield Properties LLC is a New Jersey limited liability company with its principal place of business at 1500 Commerce Drive, Suite 400, Newark, New Jersey 07102. Harold T. Greenfield serves as the registered agent for Defendant.

3. This Court has subject matter jurisdiction pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1332, as the parties are citizens of different states and the amount in controversy exceeds $75,000, exclusive of interest and costs.

4. Venue is proper in this District pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b) because the events giving rise to this claim occurred within this judicial district.

FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS

5. On March 1, 2023, Martinez entered into a residential lease agreement with Defendant for apartment Unit 3A located at 742 Elm Street, Unit 3A, Edison, New Jersey 08817, with monthly rent of $1,850.

6. The lease agreement contained express warranties that the premises would be maintained in habitable condition and that Defendant would promptly address any maintenance issues affecting the health and safety of tenants.

7. Beginning in August 2023, the Plaintiff noticed persistent water stains on the ceiling of her bedroom and bathroom, which she immediately reported to Angela Torres, the property manager employed by Defendant.

8. Despite repeated requests for repairs, Defendant failed to take adequate action to address the underlying water infiltration problem at the rental property.

9. On September 14, 2023, during a period of heavy rainfall, the ceiling in Martinez's bedroom collapsed, causing extensive water damage to her personal belongings and exposing significant black mold growth behind the drywall.

10. Angela Torres personally inspected the damaged unit on September 15, 2023, and acknowledged that the mold condition posed a serious health hazard requiring immediate remediation.

11. Rather than providing alternative housing or conducting proper mold remediation, Defendant instructed Torres to have maintenance staff simply paint over the affected areas without proper mold treatment.

12. As a direct result of prolonged exposure to toxic mold, Martinez suffered severe respiratory symptoms including persistent coughing, difficulty breathing, chest pain, and chronic fatigue beginning in late September 2023.

13. She was forced to seek emergency medical treatment at Robert Wood Johnson University Hospital on October 3, 2023, where physicians diagnosed her with mold-induced asthma and respiratory inflammation.

14. Martinez's medical expenses related to the mold exposure have totaled $8,347 as of the filing of this Complaint, including emergency room visits, specialist consultations, prescription medications, and ongoing pulmonary function monitoring.

15. The uninhabitable conditions forced the Plaintiff to vacate the premises on October 15, 2023, and secure alternative housing at significantly higher cost, resulting in additional damages of $2,200 per month in excess rent.

16. Despite vacating due to Defendant's breach of habitability warranties, Martinez was required to continue paying rent through the end of her lease term in February 2024.

COUNT I - BREACH OF WARRANTY OF HABITABILITY

17. Plaintiff incorporates by reference all preceding paragraphs as if fully set forth herein.

18. Under New Jersey law, landlords impliedly warrant that rental premises will be maintained in habitable condition throughout the tenancy.

19. Defendant breached this warranty by failing to maintain the rental property in habitable condition and by failing to promptly remedy known defects that materially affected Martinez's health and safety.

20. As a proximate result of Defendant's breach, Martinez suffered significant damages including medical expenses, relocation costs, and personal property damage.

COUNT II - NEGLIGENCE

21. Plaintiff incorporates by reference all preceding paragraphs as if fully set forth herein.

22. Defendant owed a duty to Martinez to maintain the rental property in a reasonably safe condition and to exercise reasonable care in performing maintenance and repairs.

23. Defendant breached this duty by failing to properly investigate and remediate the water infiltration and mold contamination despite actual knowledge of the dangerous condition.

24. Defendant's negligent maintenance practices directly and proximately caused Martinez's injuries and damages.

PRAYER FOR RELIEF

WHEREFORE, Plaintiff respectfully requests that this Court enter judgment in her favor and against Defendant as follows:

B. Punitive damages for Defendant's willful and wanton disregard for tenant safety;

C. Reasonable attorneys' fees and costs of suit;

D. Such other relief as this Court deems just and proper.
