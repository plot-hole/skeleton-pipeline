**INTRODUCTION**

This complaint is filed by Jennifer Marie Hutchinson, District Attorney for the Eastern District of Michigan, located at 211 West Fort Street, Detroit, MI 48226. The defendant, Marcus Anthony Delacroix, SSN 472-89-3651, residing at 1847 Corktown Avenue, Detroit, MI 48216, telephone (313) 555-7429, is charged with violations of federal wire fraud statutes in connection with a fraudulent investment scheme that operated from January 15, 2023, through September 22, 2023.

**STATEMENT OF PROBABLE CAUSE**

**Background Investigation**

On October 3, 2023, I was assigned to investigate complaints regarding a fraudulent investment operation allegedly conducted by Delacroix. The primary victim, Catherine Rose Pemberton, residing at 42871 Maple Ridge Drive, Bloomfield Hills, MI 48304, telephone (248) 555-9162, email cpemberton.invest@gmail.com, reported losses totaling $347,850 to our department on September 28, 2023.

**The Fraudulent Scheme**

Based on my investigation, Delacroix operated under the business name "Detroit Prime Capital Investments" and solicited investments from victims through a combination of cold calls, email communications, and in-person meetings. The defendant represented that he was a licensed investment advisor with twenty years of experience managing portfolios for high-net-worth individuals. These representations were false.

Ms. Pemberton was first contacted by the defendant on January 15, 2023, via telephone at her residence. Delacroix told her that he had obtained her contact information through a referral from a mutual acquaintance and that she had been selected for an exclusive investment opportunity. He claimed that his fund specialized in cryptocurrency arbitrage and guaranteed returns of 15-20% quarterly.

**Wire Communications in Furtherance of the Scheme**

During the period from January 15, 2023, through September 22, 2023, Delacroix sent numerous emails to Pemberton from the address mdelacroix.dpc@tempmail.org. In these electronic communications, he provided false account statements, fabricated performance reports, and continued to solicit additional investments. The defendant used interstate wire communications to transmit these fraudulent materials, knowing they contained material misrepresentations.

On February 12, 2023, Pemberton wired $125,000 from her account at First Michigan Bank to an account controlled by Delacroix at Detroit Commerce Bank, account ending in 7429. She made additional wire transfers of $89,200 on April 18, 2023, and $133,650 on July 9, 2023, for a total investment of $347,850.

**Discovery of the Fraud**

The scheme began to unravel in September 2023, when Pemberton requested a partial withdrawal of her funds to cover unexpected medical expenses. Delacroix initially delayed her request, claiming that funds were temporarily tied up in a large arbitrage transaction. When she persisted, he stopped responding to her calls and emails entirely.

Pemberton's concern led her to contact a minor witness, J.T.M., age 15, who had overheard conversations between the defendant and another individual discussing the fictitious nature of the investment accounts. The minor witness was related to an individual who had previously worked with Delacroix and had knowledge of his fraudulent activities. J.T.M. provided crucial information that helped establish the defendant's intent to defraud investors.

**Investigation and Evidence**

My investigation revealed that Delacroix had no securities licenses, no legitimate investment business, and no ability to generate the returns he promised. Bank records obtained through subpoena showed that the funds received from Pemberton were not invested as represented but were instead used by the defendant for personal expenses, including payments on his residence at 1847 Corktown Avenue, luxury vehicle purchases, and gambling activities.

Computer forensics analysis of Delacroix's devices revealed the creation of false investment statements using basic spreadsheet software. Email records confirmed that he had sent similar solicitations to at least fifteen other potential victims across multiple states, establishing the interstate nature of his scheme.

**Arrest and Charges**

On November 15, 2023, I arrested Marcus Anthony Delacroix at his residence without incident. At the time of his arrest, he was found in possession of additional fraudulent investment documents and contact lists for prospective victims.

**STATUTORY VIOLATIONS**

Based on the foregoing facts, I believe that Delacroix violated 18 U.S.C. § 1343 (Wire Fraud) by devising and executing a scheme to defraud victims of money through materially false and fraudulent pretenses, representations, and promises, and by transmitting communications in interstate commerce in furtherance of the scheme.

Additionally, the defendant violated 18 U.S.C. § 1341 (Mail Fraud) through his use of the United States Postal Service to deliver fraudulent investment documents and correspondence to victims as part of the same scheme to defraud.

**CONCLUSION**

Wherefore, I request that a warrant be issued for the arrest of Marcus Anthony Delacroix and that he be held to answer these charges in accordance with law.
