**FACTUAL BACKGROUND**

On the morning of 1/5/2024, our client was walking through the parking lot of the above-referenced commercial property when he slipped and fell on black ice that had formed due to a broken water pipe. The defendant corporation had actual notice of the dangerous condition, as evidenced by multiple prior complaints from tenants and customers. Despite this knowledge, Pacific Northwest Insurance Corporation failed to take reasonable steps to remedy the hazardous situation or provide adequate warnings to pedestrians.

Mr. Rodriguez sustained serious injuries including a fractured left wrist, severe bruising to his hip and back, and a mild concussion. He was transported by ambulance to Sacred Heart Medical Center where he received initial emergency treatment on January 8, 2024. Follow-up treatment continued through 02/15/2024, requiring multiple visits to orthopedic specialists and physical therapy.

**LIABILITY**

Your insured's liability is clear under Oregon premises liability law. As established in Peterson v. Cascade Properties, 156 Or. App. 234 (1998), property owners owe a duty of reasonable care to invitees. The defendant corporation breached this duty by:

1. Failing to inspect the premises regularly for dangerous conditions;
2. Failing to repair the broken water pipe in a timely manner;
3. Failing to provide adequate warnings of the icy conditions; and
4. Failing to apply de-icing materials despite forecasted freezing temperatures.

The statutory framework governing premises liability, effective January 1, 2020, under O.R.S. 31.185, establishes clear standards that your insured violated. The plaintiff has complied with all notice requirements and brings this claim well within the two-year period under O.R.S. 12.110.
