PLAINTIFF'S MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT

TO THE HONORABLE COURT:

NOW COMES Plaintiff Michelle Patricia Rodriguez, by and through her undersigned counsel, and hereby respectfully moves this Honorable Court for summary judgment in her favor on all claims pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 56. In support of this Motion, Plaintiff states as follows:

I. INTRODUCTION

This employment discrimination action arises under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, 42 U.S.C. § 2000e et seq. Plaintiff Rodriguez, a Latina woman, was subjected to a hostile work environment and unlawful termination based on her race, national origin, and sex during her employment with Defendant Southeastern Industrial Solutions, Inc. The undisputed material facts establish that Defendant engaged in discriminatory conduct that violates federal law, entitling Ms. Rodriguez to judgment as a matter of law.

II. STATEMENT OF UNDISPUTED MATERIAL FACTS

1. Plaintiff Michelle Patricia Rodriguez, residing at 4729 Peachtree Boulevard, Atlanta, GA 30309, was employed by Defendant from January 8, 2022, through her termination on September 14, 2023.

2. Rodriguez worked as a Senior Quality Control Supervisor at Defendant's manufacturing facility located at 8850 Corporate Center Drive, Charlotte, NC 28226.

3. Throughout her employment, she was subjected to discriminatory comments regarding her ethnicity and gender by her direct supervisor, Thomas Kenneth Hartwell, and plant manager David Charles Morrison.

4. On March 15, 2024, Rodriguez was deposed and testified under oath regarding the hostile work environment she endured, including being called "spicy" and "fiery" in reference to her Latina heritage, as documented in Exhibit 1.

5. Defendant's own personnel records, attached as Exhibit 2, demonstrate that Rodriguez consistently received positive performance evaluations and exceeded production targets.

6. Internal company emails dated between July 2023 and September 2023, produced as Exhibit 3, reveal discriminatory animus on the part of management toward Rodriguez.

7. On August 28, 2023, Rodriguez filed a formal complaint with Defendant's Human Resources Department regarding the hostile work environment.

8. Less than three weeks later, Defendant terminated Rodriguez's employment, citing alleged performance issues that are contradicted by her documented record of excellence.

9. Witness statements from co-workers Maria Elena Santos and Robert James Mitchell, attached as Exhibit 4, corroborate the discriminatory treatment experienced by the plaintiff.

10. Expert testimony from Dr. Sarah Louise Pemberton, a workplace discrimination specialist, as detailed in Exhibit 5, establishes that the circumstances surrounding Rodriguez's termination demonstrate unlawful retaliation.

III. LEGAL STANDARD

Summary judgment is appropriate when "there is no genuine dispute as to any material fact and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law." Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(a). The Court must view all evidence in the light most favorable to the non-moving party and draw all reasonable inferences in their favor.

IV. ARGUMENT

A. Defendant Is Liable for Creating and Maintaining a Hostile Work Environment

To establish a hostile work environment claim under Title VII, a plaintiff must demonstrate that she was subjected to unwelcome conduct based on a protected characteristic that was sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter the terms and conditions of employment. Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., 510 U.S. 17 (1993).

The undisputed facts establish that Rodriguez was repeatedly subjected to ethnicity-based and gender-based comments by supervisory personnel. Hartwell's documented statements referring to her as "the spicy one" and Morrison's comment that "women like her are too emotional for leadership roles" clearly constitute discrimination based on protected characteristics. These incidents occurred regularly over an eighteen-month period, creating an objectively hostile work environment that no reasonable employee should be required to endure.

B. Defendant Unlawfully Retaliated Against Rodriguez

Title VII prohibits retaliation against employees who oppose discriminatory practices. 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-3(a). The temporal proximity between Rodriguez's August 28, 2023 HR complaint and her September 14, 2023 termination, combined with the pretextual nature of Defendant's stated reasons, establishes unlawful retaliation as a matter of law.

Defendant's claim that Rodriguez was terminated for performance issues is belied by her consistently excellent evaluations and her documented achievements in exceeding quality control metrics. The company's own records demonstrate that she was a valued employee until she complained about discrimination.

V. DAMAGES

Plaintiff seeks compensatory damages for lost wages, emotional distress, and other pecuniary losses in the amount of $185,000. She also seeks punitive damages to deter future discriminatory conduct. These amounts fall within the $300,000 statutory cap under 42 U.S.C. § 1981a and satisfy the $75,000 amount-in-controversy requirement for federal jurisdiction.

VI. CONCLUSION

The undisputed material facts establish Defendant's liability for employment discrimination and retaliation. No reasonable jury could find otherwise. Plaintiff respectfully requests that this Court grant her Motion for Summary Judgment and enter judgment in her favor.
